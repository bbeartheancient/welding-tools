You’ll see this number next to the symbol, often on the left. Weld length shows up on the right side of the symbol. If a fillet weld shows “3” on the right, you’re making a 3-inch weld. Pitch or spacing tells you how far apart to put multiple welds if they aren’t continuous. It’s another number on the right, after the length. So, a weld length of 2 inches with a pitch of 5 inches means you make 2-inch welds spaced 5 inches apart. ### **Supplementary Symbols: Contour, All‑Around, Finishing** Supplementary symbols give more detail about weld shape and finish. A contour symbol tells you if the weld should be flat, convex, or concave. The “all-around” symbol—a circle around the reference line or arrow—means weld all the way around the joint. Finishing symbols let you know how to clean up the weld. You’ll see letters for grinding, machining, or hammering, usually next to the tail of the symbol. These symbols help you get welds that look right and meet the specs. ## **Comparison: AWS vs ISO Weld Symbol Charts** When you work with welding drawings, you’ll notice two main symbol systems: one used mostly in the U.S., another common in Europe and elsewhere. These systems handle weld details, sizes, and positions a bit differently, which can affect how you read and use the symbols. ### **ANSI/AWS A2.4 Chart Basics (U.S. Standard)** The AWS A2.4 chart uses a single solid reference line for welding symbols. If you need to weld on the arrow side, the symbol sits below the line. For the other side, you’ll see it above—no dashed lines here. Fillet weld dimensions are in inches, shown by leg length, often called *‘z’*. AWS symbols skip the process number in the tail. You’ll mostly see this system in North America, parts of Asia, and the Gulf. ### **ISO 2553 International Symbols & Broken‑Line Use** ISO 2553 uses two lines: one solid, one dashed. The dashed line marks welds on the *other* side of the joint. Your symbol’s spot on these lines tells you if you’re welding on the arrow side or not. Here, fillet weld sizes are given as throat thickness (*‘a’*) in millimeters, not leg length.
